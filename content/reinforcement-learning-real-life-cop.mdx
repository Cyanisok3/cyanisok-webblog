## Intro

Quay Crane (QC) / Yard Crane (YC) assignment & scheduling issues

They are typically Combinatorial Optimization Problems (COPs) and most are NP-hard

Mathematical model-driven approaches (formulate) → optimization algorithms (solve the model)

To solve COPs, there are:

1. Exact algorithms (prune the solution space, ensuring optimality -: exponential time complexity)
2. Approximation algorithms (need to adapt the problem – NFL theorem, -: )

**callback:** traditional model-driven approaches are vulnerable to the uncertain factors in the non-derterministic env. → substantial losses to generate the solutions

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms may work**

Integration of deep learning and RL (DRL)

RL ≈ an approximation of Dynamic Programming (DP) → divide-and-conquer for COPs → decompose in a recursive way & solve each part & combine these results systematically to obtain an overall solution

DP therefore suitable for the problems with overlapping/recursive function calls & depending on current&past states (no future)

In RL, use Markov Decision Process (MDP) to resolve the problems (for sequential decision making)

MDP: only current states, no past states

DP applicable problems → applicable using MDP

Hence RL can help finding solutions for COPs （partly solvable by DP, can be formulation references）

Objectives in COPs are deterministic → simple reward mechanisms

DRL is proved promising to solve COPs

**How to train RL agent in the real-world env.? (time to train & migratability in real world)**

Use Real2Sim transferring (building high-fidelity simulators from real-world data)

**Problem:** Truck dispatching optimization (spec ver. of Pickup and Delivery Problem, PDP)

**Approach:** DRL + AnyLogic simulation

**Contributions:**

1. a flexible truck dispatching system (with IoT capabilities to capture real-time data)
2. a tailor-made neural network structure (with RL agent to use state vectors)
3. simulation visualization

The proposed DRL-based simulation optimization: Explainable AI

## Problem Description and Formulation

- Berth & QC
- Trucks
- Storage Yard & YC

QC & YC: one task each time

QC: load OCs (LOAD) & Unload OCs (DISPATCH)

Yards are divided into yard blocks are divided into bays

1 YC : 1 yard block

1 QC : x tasks;

1 task : 1 truck

pre-defined order

**A task instruction:**

| Id | Source | Destination | Type | Source Bay | Destination Bay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | QC6 | 32 | DISCHARGE | 15 | 12 |
| 2 | 5 | QC2 | LOAD | 20 | 23 |

1. Move to source Bay xx of Yard Block xx
2. Pick up container after waiting for [1] the queuing & [2] the crane service time
3. Move to destination Bay xx of Yard Block xx
4. Unload the container after waiting for [1] & [2]
5. Request a new task when completing

Task for the QC needs to be removed from the list when finished

And the scheduler needs to select a new task for a truck based on all states of QCs, YCs and trucks

**Objective:** To minimize the the summation of all QC’s idle time in its QC makespan

### Problem Formulation: Mathematical Notations

- QCs nodes: $Q$
- YCs nodes: $Y$
- depot node (the starting point of trucks): $0$
- a directed graph: $G(N, A)$, where $N = \{0\} \cup Q \cup Y$, $A$ is the edges (road network) connecting nodes
- trucks: $V$, each truck: $v$

Assume truck movement over G follows the shortest path rule

- a task list for each quay crane q: $L^q$ (each task is of 2 types: loading & unloading)
- the set of all tasks: $W = \bigcup_{q \in Q} L_q$, each task: $w_i^q$
- the assignment var: $x(w_i^q, v)$: == 1, if task $w_i^q$ is assigned to $v$
- the timetamp $T_i^q$, denotingthe time that the i-th task of QC q is served by q
- the operation time $Q_i^q$ ($> 0$)
- $z(k, l)$: binary var, indicating whether task l is serviced immediately after task k by any truck
- $D(w_i^q, v)$: the time duration that the truck v takes to reach the target QC for task $w_i^q$

$\alpha(q) = 1$, if q is for loading task; $=0$, if q is for unloading task

- $T^{w_i^q, y}_{\mathrm{comp}}$ & $T^{w_i^q, y}_{\mathrm{arr}}$: completion & arrival time at target yard y for task $w_i^q$
- $H(w_i^q, v)$: the queuing time at the QC ($\ge 0$)

With the variables defined above, you need:

1. The objective function: in this case, we need to minimize the summation of all OC’s idle time considering: 1) truck’s traveling time; 2) truck’s queuing time; 3) the service time of QCs & YCs; 4) also considering the idle time at the start of the operations
2. To further expand the critical variables in your objective function (and maybe you need to define more variables to achieve it, to clarify the scope)
3. The constraints to ensure some prerequisites: in this case, to ensure the completion of all tasks, the mapping relationships (e.g. 1 task : 1 truck), the order of tasks, first-come-first-serve (FCFS) rule, etc….

## The Proposed DRL Approach

**How to apply the RL framework to the truck dispatching scenario?**

We need to model in the framework of MDP, to define:

- **Environment** (using Real2Sim simulation to help model the real-life env.)

  The purpose of Real2Sim simulation (based on AnyLogic):

  1. Validate the mathematical model, help verify that the model can work; but not represent the model is close to the real-life environment
  2. Predict the consequences of the action policies to gain complete training data (trajectories); [interative training procedure]
  3. Can be used as the core of automatic testbed, to iterate neural network, features (math model), training measures and heuristics to choose (for imitation learning); but still need isolated test cases that resemble real-life conditions

- **State**
- **Actions**
- **Reward**
- **State Transition**

### Policy Network Topology & Imitation Learning

- The set of the state vector of each QC can be dynamic, given that the task lists for QCs varies dynamically
- The expert network (parameters) is freezed (pre-trained) when conducting the formal RL training

### PPO for truck dispatching optimization

- PPO compares old and new action probabilities, and use clipping mechanizm to weaken the incentive for further updates when the change becomes substantial

## Experiment Design for Benchmarking

### Generalization - unseen problem instances

The testing instance design follows the principle of control variables

Change one of the following:

- num of trucks
- num of tasks
- num of QCs
- stacking mode (mixed & seperated loading/unloading containers)
- yard distribution (centralized & distributed: loading/unloading containers in 1 or 2 yards / in > 2 yards)

**Observation:** the performance of the proposed DRL method ~ the truck-QC ratio

Use base configuration (60 trucks, 400 tasks, 16 QCs, mixed, distributed) to train the dispatching policy and test it with a set of unseen configurations

### Generalization - unknown uncertainties

**Uncertain Factors:** Truck speed at different areas & crane operation time

The truck speed is assumed to be constant in the training, so in the testing env. this is an unknown uncertainty
